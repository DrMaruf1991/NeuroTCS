What NeuroTCS does

NeuroTCS audits longitudinal disease-state trajectories — it follows each subject across their visits and checks whether the sequence of clinical stages (CN → MCI → AD) is consistent with published staging rules (NIA-AA 2018). It does not measure, diagnose, or segment anything; it audits staging data you already have.

Because it audits trajectories over time, your data must be longitudinal: multiple visits per subject, in a known time order.

The three columns NeuroTCS must find

Your file needs at least these three pieces of information (column names can differ — you map them in the UI after upload):

RoleWhat it isExample
subject idA stable identifier for each person (same across their visits)S001, PT-4471
visit date or visitWhen each assessment happened, or its order. Optional — order is derived from row order if omitted.2019-03-14, or 1, 2, 3
stateThe clinical stage at that visitCN, MCI, AD

The subject id must be identical across a person's visits — that's how visits are grouped into one trajectory (watch for trailing spaces or S1 vs s1).

The state column: what's accepted

✔ Already-labelled states (simplest — always works)

CN (cognitively normal), MCI (mild cognitive impairment), AD (dementia). The sub-stages SMC, EMCI, LMCI are also accepted.

✔ Common text synonyms (auto-normalized — the checkbox is on by default)

Your labelBecomes
Normal, Cognitively Normal, NC, CUCN
early MCIEMCI
Alzheimer's disease, Alzheimer's dementiaAD

Every substitution is shown in the result ("Normalized stage labels: …") — nothing changes silently.

✕ Numeric codes — DON'T upload raw numbers

Convert numeric staging codes to CN/MCI/AD text before uploading. A column of raw numbers (1/2/3, CDR-global 0/0.5/1, NACCUDSD, …) is not crosswalked using your cohort's meaning. The engine may instead match the numbers to a different published numeric scheme (e.g. NIA-AA 2024 clinical staging, where 1/2/3 mean preclinical / transitional / MCI-like) and return a plausible-looking but wrong score. For example, ADNI's DXCURREN codes are 1=Normal, 2=MCI, 3=AD — an upload of those raw codes is misinterpreted and a real reversal (3→2→1) is not flagged.

Convert first, e.g. CDR-global 0 → CN, 0.5 → MCI, ≥1 → AD; ADNI 1 → CN, 2 → MCI, 3 → AD. (For a recognized public cohort, use the CLI's --cohort path, which applies that cohort's exact crosswalk.)

✕ Bare Dementia is not recognized

The ADNIMERGE DX label Dementia is not mapped (it can mean non-AD dementia). Use AD or Alzheimer's disease, or map Dementia → AD before uploading. The finer ADNI stages SMC, EMCI, LMCI are recognized.

How unmappable states are handled (important)

NeuroTCS is an auditor — it surfaces data problems rather than silently hiding them. A value in state that doesn't resolve to CN/MCI/AD is treated one of two ways:

Your valueWhat happens
blank / empty / missingthat single visit is dropped; the trajectory joins the visits on either side (no flag)
a non-null value that isn't a stage (Unknown, 8, -99, a typo)it is kept as a state, and the transitions into and out of it are flagged impossible
So one stray Unknown in a subject's timeline produces two flagged transitions (… → Unknown and Unknown → …). That's the auditor doing its job. If those aren't real findings, map the value to CN/MCI/AD or remove the row before uploading.

Requirements checklist

  • Longitudinal — at least some subjects have 2+ visits. One row per subject (cross-sectional) has no trajectory to audit → no cTCS.
  • Consistent IDs — a subject's ID is identical across their visits.
  • Orderable visits — a date or visit number per visit (or accept the derived-from-row-order note).
  • Mappable states — CN/MCI/AD or a recognized synonym; numeric scores pre-converted (see above).
  • One staging table — a single sheet with these columns. A .zip may hold several files, but multi-file cohort joins (like MIRIAD) are handled by the cohort loaders, not the generic upload.

What each message means (and the fix)

  • "mapping incomplete: 'sheet', 'subject_id' and 'state' are required" — pick a subject id and a state column in the dropdowns before running.
  • "the staging layer did not run — check that 'state' points at a recognized staging column" — your state values didn't resolve to CN/MCI/AD. Turn on "Normalize stage labels", or (for numeric scores) pre-convert to CN/MCI/AD.
  • "mapping does not match the data" — a chosen column isn't in the sheet (e.g. wrong sheet). Re-check the dropdowns.
  • "…has no visit column; visit index was DERIVED from … order" (a note, not an error) — some/all visits had no date; order came from row order. Provide dates or a visit index for a guaranteed-correct order.
  • cTCS = 1.0 with very few transitions — almost no state changes. Valid, but confirm the data really is longitudinal with some progression.
  • Very high "flagged" rate — many transitions violate the staging rules. That's a real finding, not an error — inspect the flagged transitions.

What a flag means

A flag marks a transition that is inadmissible or improbable under the cited rule pack. Every flag requires adjudication: it is not, by itself, a verdict of data error. A flagged transition may turn out to be (1) a true data error, (2) real but rare biology — e.g. a documented diagnostic reclassification, resolved delirium or depression, or a medication effect — or (3) an artifact of how the dataset's labels were mapped into the rule pack's states. Severity tier names (impossible / implausible / informational) are stable machine-schema tokens, not clinical verdicts. The fraction of flags that are true data errors is measured only by the blinded expert-adjudication study specified in docs/VALIDATION_PROTOCOL.md.

Privacy

  • The upload is your own file, processed transiently and discarded when the audit returns — nothing is stored server-side.
  • Subject ids are hashed before the audit runs, so the results, the flagged table, and the downloadable bundle carry only stable, non-reversible hashes — never your raw ids.
  • Still, upload de-identified data only — no names, no MRNs, no dates of birth. Coded subject ids are fine (and are hashed in the output).
