## Framework 1 — Riley 2024 sample-size methodology

**Citation (verified):**
> Riley RD, Snell KIE, Archer L, Ensor J, Debray TPA, Van Calster B, van Smeden M,
> Collins GS. Evaluation of clinical prediction models (part 3): calculating the
> sample size required for an external validation study.
> *BMJ* 2024;384:e074821.

- **PMID**: 38253388
- **PMCID**: PMC11778934
- **DOI**: 10.1136/bmj-2023-074821
- **Published**: 22 January 2024
- **License**: CC-BY 4.0 (open, commercial OK)
- **Software**: `pmvalsampsize` R/Stata package at prognosisresearch.com/software

### Verified facts (locked)

| Item | Verified value |
|---|---|
| Continuous outcome — number of criteria | **4** (R², calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, residual variance) |
| Binary outcome — number of criteria | **4** (O/E statistic, calibration slope, c-statistic, net benefit) |
| Time-to-event approach | Simulation-based (no closed-form) |
| Worked example 1 (pain intensity SVM) | N=886 (R²), 184 (CITL), 258 (cal slope), 235 (residual var) → **max 886** |
| Worked example 2 (ISARIC 4C COVID) | N=423 (O/E), 949 (cal slope), 347 (c-stat), 38–407 (net benefit by threshold) → **max 949** |
| Missing-data inflation | N/(1−x), e.g. 949/0.95 = 999 |
| C-statistic SE formula author | Newcombe (2006) |
| Net benefit SE formula author | Marsh et al. (2020), DOI 10.1111/biom.13190 |

### Critical scope note for NeuroTCS

The Riley framework is for **external-validation precision** (Aims 1, 2, 3 of
NeuroTCS), **not** for audit-time cohort sizing. NeuroTCS v1.7.0 docs explicitly
disclaim this distinction. Tests validate against the published worked-example
numbers (886, 184, 258, 235, 423, 949, 347) within ±2 tolerance for integer
rounding behaviour.

### 5b. Silent trial methodology

**Citation (verified):**
> Kwong JCC, Erdman L, Khondker A, Skreta M, Goldenberg A, McCradden MD,
> Lorenzo AJ, Rickard M. The silent trial - the bridge between bench-to-bedside
> clinical AI applications. *Front Digit Health* 2022;4:929508.

- **PMID**: 36052317
- **PMCID**: PMC9424628
- **DOI**: 10.3389/fdgth.2022.929508
- **Published**: 16 August 2022
- **License**: CC-BY 4.0 (open, commercial OK — verbatim Table 1 reproduction
  permitted with citation)
- **Institution**: Toronto SickKids + University of Toronto Temerty Centre
  for AI Research and Education in Medicine

### Verified definition (verbatim)

> "the silent trial, which evaluates the proposed model on patients in real-time,
> while the end-users (i.e., clinicians) are blinded to predictions such that
> they do not influence clinical decision-making."

### Verified 3-stage roadmap (verbatim)

> "a 3-stage roadmap for the evaluation and validation of AI models into clinical
> care… These phases include (1) exploratory model development, (2) a silent
> trial, and (3) prospective clinical evaluation."

### Verified Table 1 — 4 major silent-trial themes (encoded in NeuroTCS)

1. **Dataset drift** — changes between training and silent-trial patients
   (data definitions, demographics, clinical practice)
2. **Bias** — was the model trained on a generalizable dataset; which subsets
   benefit vs. are harmed
3. **Feasibility** — workflow integration time, UI simplicity, computing
   resources
4. **Stakeholder attitudes** — patient and clinician acceptance, alignment with
   care priorities

### Case-study performance trajectory (verbatim from paper)

> "Initial AUROC 0.90 → silent trial AUROC 0.50 → after refinement AUROC 0.85
> (Silent Trial 1) → AUROC 0.91 (Silent Trial 2)."

This precipitous drop to chance-level performance during silent trial is the
canonical justification for why NeuroTCS exists as a separate evaluation layer
before active clinical deployment.

### Supplementary recent review (2026)

- **Scoping review**: *Nat Health* 2026, DOI 10.1038/s44360-025-00048-z —
  "A scoping review of silent trials for medical artificial intelligence."
  891 articles screened, 75 included. Provides contemporary state-of-the-art.

## Cross-reference 4 — EU AI Act Article 72

**Citation (verified):**
> Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council laying
> down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence (Artificial Intelligence Act),
> Article 72 — Post-market monitoring by providers and post-market monitoring
> plan for high-risk AI systems.

- **Chapter**: IX (Post-Market Monitoring, Information Sharing and Market
  Surveillance), Section 1 (Post-Market Monitoring)
- **License**: EU public document
- **Implementing-act deadline**: 2 February 2026 (Commission to adopt template
  for post-market monitoring plan)

### Verified verbatim paragraphs

**Paragraph 1:**
> "Providers shall establish and document a post-market monitoring system in a
> manner that is proportionate to the nature of the AI technologies and the
> risks of the high-risk AI system."

**Paragraph 2:**
> "The post-market monitoring system shall actively and systematically collect,
> document and analyse relevant data…"

**Paragraph 4:**
> "For high-risk AI systems covered by the Union harmonisation legislation
> listed in Section A of Annex I, where a post-market monitoring system and plan
> are already established under that legislation, in order to ensure
> consistency, avoid duplications and minimise additional burdens, providers
> shall have a choice of integrating, as appropriate, the necessary elements
> described in paragraphs 1, 2 and 3 using the template referred in paragraph 3
> into systems and plans already existing under that legislation, provided that
> it achieves an equivalent level of protection."

### Application to medical AI

Per Annex III point 5 and standalone MDR classification, medical AI in the EU
is high-risk by default; therefore Article 72 applies to virtually all
NeuroTCS users in the EU. Paragraph 4 is the integration clause — EU AI Act
PMS plans for medical AI can be integrated with MDR Article 83 vigilance
requirements, avoiding duplication.
